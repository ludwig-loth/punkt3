**Metavis** is an editor for the guided creation of metaphorical visualizations. Users can link data sets to specific visual elements of a topic. The resulting visualization updates in real time based on changes in the source data and provides a picture of the data that is rarely found in visualizations.

Users can thus present and read their data in a visually appealing form, while at the same time it appears to the uninitiated as a standard image. That's why I recommend displaying the visualization window on a screen that hangs on the wall and is permanently in operation. A kind of digital picture frame that updates dynamically and in real time based on user data and thus ideally looks different every day. This also allows users to identify and track trends in their data.

Metavis was created as part of my master's thesis and is almost entirely self-made. This means that both the front and back ends were designed and implemented independently. In addition, I planned and carried out an expert-based evaluation of the tool.

## Metaphorical Visualization

![Exemplary visualization generated from Metavis](/images/projects/metavis/block-undefined.png)

The figure shows an example of such a visualization. All elements were created/drawn by me.

The data can be linked to individual elements and changes dynamically. For example, flowers can wilt or change color, trees can grow or shrink, and other characteristics can change.

## My Role

![](/images/projects/metavis/block-undefined.png)

I went through the entire software engineering cycle for this project. From the idea, through planning, analysis, and implementation, to evaluation and revision.

This project involved several realignments and new beginnings. It was a very big task for me as a solo developer and far too ambitious for a master's thesis, but I was passionate about it and wanted to implement it and complete it according to my vision.

What I learned from this in any case: Despite my years of experience with JavaScript, I started learning TypeScript after completing the project. This would have helped me a lot in handling the project.

I also learned that projects of this magnitude thrive on discussion and revision with other people. Different opinions and perspectives are essential for the success and robust functionality and UX of such a large project.
